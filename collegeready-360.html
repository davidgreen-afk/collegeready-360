A self-study for a parent and a soon-to-be college student to work through together — 15 skills, one shared answer for each, talked out loud rather than scored separately.

Purpose

Go through all 15 skills together and agree on one honest rating for each, rather than each of you scoring it alone. The conversation you have while deciding on an answer is the actual point — the results page just gives you a place to land afterward and a short list of what's worth working on before move-in day.

⏱ 10–15 minutes, done together ☰ 15 skills, one shared rating each 🔒 No login, nothing saved or sent anywhere

How this works

For each of the 15 skills below, read the statement out loud and talk until you both agree on one rating, from Strongly Disagree to Strongly Agree. Disagreement is normal and useful — it usually means you're each holding a different piece of evidence. Work it out, then pick the answer you both land on.

This is a self-report tool, not a clinical or academic assessment. It's meant to start a conversation, not settle one.

    What a self-study like this can and can't tell you

    This gives you a shared starting point and a short list of what to work on, nothing more. It can't tell you whether a low score reflects a real gap, a bad week, or just an area neither of you has tested yet. That distinction, and what to actually do about it, is where a closer, structured conversation helps.
